# Market Analysis Template

**Purpose**: Daily/weekly market commentary that builds audience and drives engagement  
**Post Type**: Threads (short, 3-4 parts) + Instagram (stories + reels)  
**Frequency**: 3-5x per week  
**Tone**: Observational, data-driven, contrarian when appropriate  

---

## TEMPLATE STRUCTURE

### PART 1: HOOK (The Single Observation)

**Formula**: Unexpected market behavior + counter to consensus + implication

```
"Most traders today think [CONSENSUS].
But look at [DATA/CHART].
[DATA] says something different."
```

**Examples**:

```
"Everyone thinks the rally is fake.
But look at breadth. 87% of stocks above 20-day MA.
That's not fake. That's early stage of a real move."
```

```
"Traders are panicking about VIX at 28.
But historically, when VIX is 28-35, it's the buying opportunity.
The real panic (VIX >40) happens 6 weeks later, not now."
```

```
"Fed tightening is supposed to crash markets.
But the last 3 tightening cycles, the market DID crash... 
After rates peaked, not during. We're still in the rising rate phase."
```

**Rules**:
- ✅ Start with consensus (what most people think)
- ✅ Show data that contradicts it (charts, stats)
- ✅ Make a contrarian call (what should actually happen)
- ✅ Include a specific number (87%, $25, 28 VIX)
- ✅ Keep it short (280 characters for hook)
- ❌ Don't claim certainty ("This will definitely crash")
- ❌ Don't be contrarian just to be contrarian
- ❌ Don't ignore contradicting data

### PART 2: THE DATA (Charts & Context)

**Purpose**: Show the chart/stat that proves your point

**Structure**:
- Chart or screenshot of the data
- What the data shows
- Historical context (when did we see this before?)
- What happened last time

**Example 1 (Breadth Analysis)**:

```
"PART 2: Here's the chart.

[SCREENSHOT: NYSE breadth chart showing 87% above 20-day MA]

This is market breadth. 
Shows percentage of stocks participating in the rally.

WHAT IT SHOWS:
When breadth is >85%, it means almost all stocks are rising (not just the mega-caps).
This is strong confirmation that the rally is real, not fake.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
Last time breadth was this high: February 2024 (before 10% rally)
And: January 2022 (before 8% correction)
And: March 2020 (before 30% rally from bottom)

PATTERN:
When breadth jumps above 85%, it's usually early stage of a move.
Direction varies, but the move is REAL."
```

**Example 2 (Volatility Analysis)**:

```
"PART 2: Here's the VIX chart.

[SCREENSHOT: VIX daily chart showing current level at 28]

VIX is at 28. People say this is panic.
But historically:

VIX 20-28: Mild stress (normal market)
VIX 28-35: Elevated stress (buying opportunity historically, 70% of the time)
VIX 35-50: Serious panic (real protection needed)
VIX >50: Capitulation (ultimate bottom)

LAST 4 TIMES VIX HIT 28:
1. June 2022: Dropped to 15 over next 2 months
2. Feb 2020: Spiked to 82 (but that was black swan)
3. Mar 2023: Dropped to 12 over next 3 months
4. May 2024: Dropped to 13 over next 2 months

PATTERN:
VIX at 28 = elevated, not panic
Real panic (where you should be terrified) = VIX 40-50"
```

**Rules**:
- ✅ Include actual chart/screenshot (not just words)
- ✅ Show historical precedents (when did we see this before?)
- ✅ Include what happened last time
- ✅ Let the data speak (don't oversell)
- ❌ Don't cherry-pick history (show multiple examples)
- ❌ Don't manipulate charts (use actual market data)
- ❌ Don't show opinions, show data

### PART 3: THE IMPLICATION (What It Means for Traders)

**Purpose**: Translate data into trading action

**Structure**:
- What the data suggests (direction, opportunity, risk)
- What traders should do (buy/sell/wait)
- What traders should NOT do (avoid the common mistake)
- Probability/confidence level

**Example 1 (Bullish Implication)**:

```
"PART 3: What this means:

WHAT THE DATA SUGGESTS:
- Breadth is strong (87% of stocks rising)
- Rally is real, not fake
- Early stage (breadth at these levels often leads to 5-15% moves)
- Direction: Likely continuation (but not guaranteed)

WHAT TRADERS SHOULD DO:
→ Momentum traders: Look for continuation trades (buy breakouts)
→ Value traders: Wait for pullbacks to add
→ Options traders: Consider call spreads (bullish, defined risk)
→ Short traders: Be cautious, stops are tight

WHAT TRADERS SHOULD NOT DO:
✗ Don't panic sell (breadth says strength, not weakness)
✗ Don't oversize (early stage moves can reverse, use 0.5-1% risk)
✗ Don't short into strength (dying on every dip will lose money)
✗ Don't trust just one indicator (breadth alone isn't enough)

CONFIDENCE LEVEL:
60% for continuation in next 2 weeks
Why only 60%? Because breadth can normalize without crashes.
Strength doesn't guarantee price strength.

MY PERSONAL BIAS:
I'm positioned for continuation, but with tight stops.
If breadth drops below 70%, I reassess."
```

**Example 2 (Caution Implication)**:

```
"PART 3: What this means:

WHAT THE DATA SUGGESTS:
- VIX at 28 is elevated but not panic
- Market is stressed but not capitulating
- Historical precedent: 70% of the time, VIX 28 = buying opportunity
- But: 30% of the time, it escalates to VIX 40+ (rare, but happens)

WHAT TRADERS SHOULD DO:
→ Bulls: Dips are temporary, buy the dip (but sizing matters)
→ Bears: Don't short into strength, wait for higher VIX to confirm
→ Hedgers: Long puts are expensive but protective
→ Neutral traders: Wait for VIX to pick a direction

WHAT TRADERS SHOULD NOT DO:
✗ Don't assume VIX at 28 = time to panic (historically wrong)
✗ Don't assume it's safe (30% of the time it escalates)
✗ Don't over-leverage (even if statistically bullish, tail risk exists)
✗ Don't ignore the 30% downside scenario (plan for it)

CONFIDENCE LEVEL:
60% for mean reversion in VIX (back to 15-18)
30% for escalation (VIX 40+)
10% for sideways (stays 25-30)

MY PERSONAL BIAS:
I'm leaning bullish but hedged. 
If VIX breaks 35, I reduce positions by 50%.
If VIX drops below 15, I increase exposure."
```

**Rules**:
- ✅ Translate data to action (what should traders do?)
- ✅ Include what NOT to do (most important)
- ✅ Quantify confidence (not just "bullish")
- ✅ Include personal bias/position (transparency builds trust)
- ✅ Include contingencies (what if the data reverses?)
- ❌ Don't claim certainty
- ❌ Don't give specific buy/sell signals (liability)
- ❌ Don't ignore the downside scenario
- ❌ Don't position yourself as certain

### PART 4: THE CONTRARIAN ANGLE (If Applicable)

**Purpose**: Add depth, show you're not following consensus

**Structure**:
- What consensus says
- Why consensus might be wrong
- What could prove consensus right
- Your probability estimates

**Example**:

```
"PART 4: But wait. Here's the contrarian take.

CONSENSUS SAYS:
"VIX at 28 means the market is breaking down. Short it."

WHY CONSENSUS MIGHT BE WRONG:
→ VIX at 28 is not historically panic (it's stress)
→ Consensus often shorts volatility at exactly the wrong time
→ The people saying "short it" don't understand vol term structure
→ Vol often contracts into bad news (not helpful for shorts)

WHAT COULD PROVE CONSENSUS RIGHT:
✓ Earnings disappointments (real reason to panic, not VIX level)
✓ Geopolitical shock (unexpected news, not reflected in VIX)
✓ Credit event (systemic problem, different from VIX spike)
✓ Fed surprise (suddenly they hike 150bps unannounced... won't happen)

MY PROBABILITY ESTIMATES:
- Consensus correct (vol continues up): 30%
- I'm correct (vol mean reverts): 60%
- Something else happens: 10%

DISCLAIMER:
Just because something is statistically likely doesn't mean it happens.
The market has surprised us before and will again.
Position size accordingly."
```

**Rules**:
- ✅ State consensus clearly
- ✅ Show WHY it's wrong (mechanism, not just opinion)
- ✅ Include what could prove you wrong (intellectual honesty)
- ✅ Give probabilities (quantify your confidence)
- ✅ Include disclaimer (you could be wrong)
- ❌ Don't be contrarian just to be different
- ❌ Don't dismiss valid risks
- ❌ Don't claim high confidence in uncertain situations
- ❌ Don't forget the Black Swan possibility

## ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY

**Comments You'll Get**:
- Disagreements ("Breadth doesn't predict direction!")
- Data challenges ("Your chart shows the opposite!")
- Macro takes ("But the Fed is about to crash the market!")
- Trade ideas ("So you're saying short the SPY?")

**How to Reply**:

**To disagreements**:
```
Them: "Breadth doesn't predict anything."

You: "Fair point. Breadth is not destiny, just probability.
Last time breadth was 85%+, what direction was the market 2 weeks later?
(Looking at historical % of time it went up vs. down)

Have you tested this correlation in your own data?"
```

**To data challenges**:
```
Them: "Your chart shows -breadth not +breadth."

You: "You're right, I see the confusion. Let me clarify:
[Restate the correct reading]

Thanks for catching that. Data interpretation matters."
```

**To macro takes**:
```
Them: "But the Fed is cutting rates next month, this will crash."

You: "Maybe. I don't know what the Fed will do.
But I do know historically when breadth is this high, the market usually
[statistic].

Your macro view could be right AND the short-term move could still be up.
Both can happen."
```

**To trade ideas**:
```
Them: "So you're saying short the SPY?"

You: "No, I'm not saying that. The data suggests continuation.
But trading decisions depend on YOUR risk tolerance, position sizing, timeframe.
What's your plan if you're wrong?"
```

## COMMON MARKET ANGLES

**Use these frameworks to analyze any market:**

| Angle | What to Look For | Example |
|-------|-----------------|---------|
| **Breadth** | % of stocks above moving average | >75% = strength, <25% = weakness |
| **Volatility** | VIX level, term structure | VIX 28 = elevated, 35+ = panic |
| **Technicals** | Support, resistance, trends | Support holds = bullish, breaks = bearish |
| **Sentiment** | Options flow, put/call ratio | Extreme readings = reversals |
| **Fundamentals** | Earnings, economic data | Strong earnings = fuel for rally |
| **Macro** | Rates, inflation, Fed signals | Rising rates usually headwind |
| **Seasonality** | Month, quarter, historical patterns | September often weak |
| **Sector rotation** | Which sectors leading/lagging | Tech strength vs. value weakness |

## TONE NOTES FOR MARKET ANALYSIS

**Credibility Builders**:
- Always show the data (don't just claim)
- Always show what you're wrong about (30-40% of the time)
- Always include probabilities (not certainties)
- Always include historical context (proves you're not guessing)
- Always include your personal position (transparency builds trust)

**Credibility Killers**:
- Claiming certainty ("This WILL crash")
- Cherry-picking history (only showing wins)
- Ignoring contradicting data
- Over-confidence in short-term prediction
- Being right by accident and claiming skill

**Balance**:
- 70% observation (here's what the data shows)
- 20% implication (here's what it might mean)
- 10% prediction (here's my bias, but I could be wrong)

Never invert this ratio. Never go beyond 10% prediction.
