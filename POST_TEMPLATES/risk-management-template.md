# Risk Management Template

**Purpose**: Safety-focused content that builds trust and demonstrates expertise  
**Post Type**: Threads (4-6 parts) + Instagram (carousel) + LinkedIn (article)  
**Frequency**: 2x per month  
**Tone**: Protective, data-driven, not fear-mongering

### PART 2: THE MATH (Why Position Size Matters)

**Purpose**: Show quantitatively why risking wrong amounts destroys accounts

**Structure**:
- Simple scenario (starting capital, per-trade risk)
- Probability of drawdown
- Outcome comparison
- Implication

**Example 1 (Too Much Risk)**:

```
"PART 2: Let's do the math.

Assume:
- Starting capital: $50,000
- Your strategy has 50% win rate
- Average win: +2%
- Average loss: -2%

SCENARIO 1: Risk 5% per trade
- Trade 1: Lose $2,500 (account = $47,500) [down 5%]
- Trade 2: Lose $2,375 (account = $45,125) [down 2.5% more]
- Trade 3: Lose $2,256 (account = $42,869) [down 2.5% more]
- Trade 4: Win $1,704 (account = $44,573) [up 3.7%]
- Trade 5: Lose $2,229 (account = $42,344) [down 5%]

5 trades in and you've had 1 win, 4 losses.
Your account is down 15% despite 50% win rate.
CONFIDENCE IS SHOT. Most traders quit here.

SCENARIO 2: Risk 1% per trade
- Trade 1: Lose $500 (account = $49,500) [down 1%]
- Trade 2: Lose $495 (account = $49,005) [down 0.5% more]
- Trade 3: Lose $490 (account = $48,515) [down 0.5% more]
- Trade 4: Win $302 (account = $48,817) [up 0.6%]
- Trade 5: Lose $488 (account = $48,329) [down 1%]

Same 5 trades. Different result.
You're down 3.3% instead of 15%.
You have the confidence to keep going.

DIFFERENCE BETWEEN SCENARIOS:
1% risk: You survive drawdowns, trust your strategy
5% risk: You blow up before your strategy proves itself"
```

**Example 2 (Recovery Math)**:

```
"PART 2: Here's why position size matters for recovery.

Assume: Your account drops 30% (realistic max drawdown)

STARTING: $50,000

AFTER -30%: $35,000 (you're down $15,000)

TO GET BACK TO $50,000, YOU NEED:
$50,000 / $35,000 = 1.43x return
That's a 43% GAIN from the bottom.

If your strategy made 15% annually...
43% gain would take 2.9 years to recover.

BUT:
If max drawdown was 15% instead of 30%:
After -15%: $42,500
To recover: need 17.6% gain
If strategy makes 15% annually: ~1.2 years to recover

LESSON:
Smaller drawdowns = faster recovery = more total profit over time.
Position sizing isn't about greed. It's about survival."
```

**Rules**:
- ✅ Use concrete numbers (not percentages alone)
- ✅ Show scenario comparison (same trades, different outcomes)
- ✅ Include psychological factor (confidence matters)
- ✅ Show recovery math (why it matters long-term)
- ❌ Don't oversimplify (don't claim position size guarantees wins)
- ❌ Don't ignore psychology (emotion is 50% of the battle)
- ❌ Don't skip the math (numbers are credible)

### PART 3: THE RULE (The Framework)

**Purpose**: Teach the specific risk management rule

**Structure**:
- The rule (simple, mechanical)
- How to calculate it
- Real examples
- Why it works

**Example 1 (Kelly Criterion Simplified)**:

```
"PART 3: Here's the rule I use (Kelly Criterion, simplified):

STEP 1: Know your win rate and risk/reward

Example:
- Win rate: 50%
- Average win: +2% per trade
- Average loss: -1% per trade
- Risk/reward ratio: 2:1

STEP 2: Calculate max position size

Formula: (Win% × Win$ - Loss% × Loss$) / Win$
= (0.50 × 2 - 0.50 × 1) / 2
= (1 - 0.5) / 2
= 0.5 / 2
= 0.25 = 25%

MEANING:
Risk 25% of account max on any single trade.

BUT WAIT: This is the mathematical maximum.
In practice, use HALF that: 12.5% risk.
Why? Because it's more conservative and handles variance.

SIMPLIFIED VERSION:
Can't do math on the fly?
Use this rule: Risk 1% per trade, max.
This works for almost every strategy.
Your math might say 25%, but 1% keeps you safe."
```

**Example 2 (Simple Percentage Rule)**:

```
"PART 3: The 1-2-3 Risk Rule

RULE 1: Risk 1% per trade maximum
- If account = $50,000, risk $500 per trade
- How? Size your position so a stop loss = $500
- Example: Stock at $50, stop at $40 = $10 loss per share
  Position size = $500 / $10 = 50 shares

RULE 2: Max 2 simultaneous losing trades
- Don't have more than 2 open positions in drawdown
- If you hit a 3rd loss: Close something and reassess
- Prevents spiral of losses

RULE 3: Close positions when down 3 days in a row
- Loss, loss, loss = regime change signal
- Close and evaluate your strategy
- You're seeing something your rules didn't account for

EXAMPLE:
- Position 1: Down $500 (within 1% loss)
- Position 2: Down $450 (within 1% loss)
- Position 3 set up, would be loss: DON'T ENTER
- You're already down 1.9%. Wait for a win.

This simple rule prevents account blowups 95% of the time."
```

**Rules for the Framework**:
- ✅ Make it mechanical (no judgment calls)
- ✅ Include examples (show the math)
- ✅ Start with safest version (traders can adapt once they know why)
- ✅ Explain why each rule exists
- ✅ Include edge cases (what if I'm already in 2 positions?)
- ❌ Don't make it too complex (traders won't follow it)
- ❌ Don't ignore psychology (rules must feel sustainable)
- ❌ Don't change the rule after a loss (consistency is key)

### PART 4: THE FAILURE CASE (What Happens Without It)

**Purpose**: Validate why the rule matters (horror stories)

**Structure**:
- Real (anonymized) example of rule violation
- What happened (losses)
- What could have happened (with rule)
- The lesson

**Example**:

```
"PART 4: What happens when you ignore position sizing:

REAL CASE: A trader I know (we'll call him Tom)

Starting capital: $25,000
Strategy: Momentum trading
Entry signal: 20-day high breakout
Drawdown tolerance: "I can handle 20% max"

TOM'S DECISION:
"My strategy has a 60% win rate. I'm confident. I'll risk 3% per trade."

WHAT HAPPENED:
- Trade 1: Won $1,500 (risk $750, risk was too small to feel real)
- Trade 2: Lost $750 (still fine, within plan)
- Trade 3: Won $1,500 (confidence rising: "I'm good at this")
- Trade 4: Lost $2,250 (ignored the rule, risked 9% instead of 3%)
- Trade 5: Lost $2,250 again (panic trading, risked 9% again)
- Trade 6: Lost $2,250 again (account now $17,250, down 30%)

After 6 trades:
- 3 wins, 3 losses (matches his 50% win rate... wait, he said 60%?)
- Down $7,750 (30% loss)
- Confidence destroyed

WHAT COULD HAVE HAPPENED WITH 1% RULE:
- Trade 1: Won $300 (risk $250)
- Trade 2: Lost $250 (1% loss)
- Trade 3: Won $300 (still up $300 total)
- Trade 4: Lost $250 (1% loss)
- Trade 5: Lost $250 (down 1% total)
- Trade 6: Lost $250 (down 1% total)

Same 6 trades. With 1% risk:
- Account still $24,250 (down less than 4%)
- Confidence intact
- Ready to continue

DIFFERENCE:
- With 3% rule: Account -30%, confidence gone
- With 1% rule: Account -4%, confidence intact

Tom quit at -30%. With 1% risk, he would have continued and won back the losses on trade 7-8."
```

**Rules**:
- ✅ Use real (anonymized) examples
- ✅ Show exact numbers
- ✅ Compare two scenarios
- ✅ Highlight the psychological impact
- ✅ Show recovery potential with proper sizing
- ❌ Don't name-drop people (maintain privacy)
- ❌ Don't make it sound like the person was stupid (it happens to smart traders)
- ❌ Don't claim position sizing guarantees wins

### PART 5: THE ADJUSTMENTS (When to Tighten/Loosen)

**Purpose**: Show risk management is dynamic, not static

**Structure**:
- Base rule (what we started with)
- When to reduce risk (tighten)
- When to increase risk (carefully)
- Decision framework

**Example**:

```
"PART 5: Risk management is dynamic.

BASE RULE: Risk 1% per trade

TIGHTEN RISK WHEN:
✓ You're in a drawdown (account down >10%)
  → Reduce to 0.5% until you recover
✓ Your win rate dropped (used to 60%, now 45%)
  → Something changed, reduce to 0.5% and investigate
✓ Market volatility spiked (VIX >30)
  → Increase position size buffer (if $500 stop, use $600 to account for gaps)
✓ A new trade type is untested (never traded this market)
  → Paper trade for 2 months, then 0.5% live, then 1% after 20 wins

LOOSEN RISK WHEN:
✗ You've had 50 consecutive wins (don't exist, but if they did...)
→ You can increase to 1.5% after proving durability
✗ You've traded for 2+ years with consistent results
→ Increase to 1.5% if strategy hasn't changed
✗ Your account grew but position size didn't scale
→ Adjust position size up, but keep % same (if account $100K, risk $1K not $500)

FRAMEWORK:
If account is healthy (within drawdown tolerance): Risk 1%
If account is hurting (>10% down): Risk 0.5%
If account is recovering (<10% down): Risk 0.5% until back to peak
If account is at all-time high: Risk 1% (but raise absolute $ to scale)

CRITICAL RULE:
Never increase risk size just because you're winning.
Never decrease win rate and keep same risk size.
Never ignore a regime change because position sizing is working."
```

**Rules**:
- ✅ Show tighten conditions (more important)
- ✅ Show loosen conditions (rare, but possible)
- ✅ Include framework (how to decide)
- ✅ Include critical dos and don'ts
- ❌ Don't make it sound like you can time volatility perfectly
- ❌ Don't suggest risk increases (most traders fail that way)
- ❌ Don't ignore the psychological aspect (comfort matters)

## TONE NOTES

**For Risk Management Content**:
- Balance protection with opportunity (don't be fear-mongering)
- Use data, not scare tactics
- Include real examples (anonymized)
- Show recovery paths (there's always a way back)
- Acknowledge that smart traders make sizing mistakes (normalize it)
- Celebrate discipline (frame proper sizing as strength, not weakness)

**Avoid**:
- Claiming risk management prevents all losses
- Oversimplifying complex risk concepts
- Ignoring that different strategies need different sizing
- Making traders feel stupid for taking risks

**Instead**:
- Show how sizing protects against psychological failures
- Explain the math clearly
- Provide frameworks they can adapt
- Celebrate traders who follow their own rules
