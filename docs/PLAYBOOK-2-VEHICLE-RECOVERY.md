# PLAYBOOK 2: Vehicle Recovery & Fix-and-Flip

## Executive Summary

**Goal**: Generate $300K/year by acquiring abandoned/old fleet vehicles for free/cheap, doing minimum repairs, and selling for $3K-$6K profit per vehicle.

**Volume Target**: 8-10 vehicles/month = $25K/month profit

**Timeline**: 
- Month 1-2: Set up sourcing channels
- Month 3-4: First batch of vehicles (5-8)
- Month 5+: Steady flow (2-3 per week)

---

## 1. VEHICLE SOURCING STRATEGY

### 1.1 Free/Cheap Vehicle Sources (Priority Order)

#### **TIER 1: Institutional Partners (PRIMARY)**
- City of Phoenix fleet retirement (5-15 vehicles/year)
- Nonprofit fleet upgrades (2-5 vehicles/year each)
- Police/fire surplus vehicles (annual auction)
- School district fleet disposal (5-10 vehicles/year)

**Approach**: 
- When signing institutional fleet contract, ask: "Do you retire old vehicles?"
- Offer: "Free removal. We handle paperwork, title transfer."
- Result: Free vehicles + strengthen client relationship

#### **TIER 2: Public Sources**

**City Auctions**
- City of Phoenix surplus auctions (quarterly)
- Maricopa County fleet sales (2x/year)
- Tempe/Chandler municipal auctions
- Typically: 3-5 vehicles at $500-$2,000 each

**Police/Fire Impound**
- Unclaimed vehicles (30-90 day hold)
- Contact: Phoenix Police Property Division
- Often sell for $200-$800
- Vehicles: Seized, abandoned, or towing hold

#### **TIER 3: Private Sources**

**Craigslist "Free" Section**
- Search: "Free car", "Free truck", "Free vehicle"
- Phoenix area: 5-10 free listings/week
- Challenge: High competition, pickup logistics
- Profit potential: $2K-$4K per vehicle

**Facebook Marketplace**
- Similar to Craigslist
- Often more negotiable (private sellers)
- Can offer: Free towing/removal
- Lower competition than Craigslist

**Estate Sales**
- Elderly homeowners, downsizing
- Often have unused/extra vehicles
- Contact: Estate sale companies in Phoenix
- Vehicles typically in good condition

**Tow Yards / Auto Salvage**
- Vehicles in storage (abandoned/unpaid tows)
- Contact local tow yards: Ask about vehicle auctions
- Typically $300-$1,500
- Salvage yards: Parts cars (focus on restorable units)

#### **TIER 4: B2B Partnerships**

**Used Car Dealers**
- Trade-in vehicles they can't sell
- Offer: "We'll take off your hands for free"
- Volume: 2-5 vehicles/month potential
- Quality: Often better condition

**Rental Car Companies**
- End-of-lease vehicles being auctioned
- Hertz, Enterprise have auctions
- Condition: Well-maintained but high mileage
- Cost: Auction prices ($2K-$4K typically)

**Insurance Companies**
- Total-loss vehicles (salvage title)
- Auctions happen monthly
- Cost: 50-70% of market value
- Profit: Buy low, flip with salvage title discount

```
FRIDAY
- [ ] Pick up vehicles (or arrange towing)
- [ ] Process paperwork (titles, registrations)
- [ ] Get vehicles to shop for assessment
```

### 2.2 Deal Evaluation Scorecard

**For each vehicle, score on:**

```
Acquisition Cost:        ___ (target: $0-$500)
Mechanical Condition:    ___ (1-10 scale, 7+ only)
Body/Interior Quality:   ___ (1-10 scale, 6+ acceptable)
Marketability:           ___ (1-10 scale, 7+ flip quickly)
Estimated Repair Cost:   ___ (target: $500-$1,500 max)
Estimated Flip Price:    ___ (target: $3,500-$6,000)

Profit Potential:        [Flip Price] - [Acquisition] - [Repairs] = ___

DECISION RULE:
- Profit > $2,000:      YES (acquire)
- Profit $1,500-$2,000: MAYBE (if low acquisition cost)
- Profit < $1,500:      NO (pass)
```

### 2.3 Acquisition Process

**Step 1: Identify**
- Find vehicle online or through relationship
- Get photos, year/make/model, mileage
- Note: Condition, running status, title status

**Step 2: Evaluate**
- Use scorecard above
- Estimate repair costs (get mechanic input if needed)
- Calculate profit

**Step 3: Negotiate**
- For free vehicles: Simple (handle removal, paperwork)
- For auction: Bid strategy (don't overpay)
- For private: Offer $200-$500 if they're motivated to sell

**Step 4: Paperwork**
- Get title transfer documents
- Verify no liens (title check)
- Check for outstanding tickets/issues
- Arrange temporary registration (if needed for transport)

**Step 5: Transport**
- Arrange towing if vehicle not running
- If running, drive to shop or use flatbed
- Take possession photos (document condition)

**Step 6: Shop Assessment**
- Mechanic does full diagnostic
- Estimate repairs needed
- Re-evaluate profit potential
- If profit margin erodes too much, consider salvage/parts-out

## 3. REPAIR STRATEGY

### 3.1 Repair Philosophy: "Minimum Viable"

**Goal**: Make vehicle pass inspection, run reliably, appeal to buyers. NOT perfection.

**Typical Repairs** (in priority order)

| Repair | Cost | Priority | Why |
|--------|------|----------|-----|
| Oil change | $45 | 1 | Makes engine run better |
| Tire replacement (if needed) | $200-400 | 1 | Safety, looks |
| Battery | $100-150 | 1 | Must start reliably |
| Brakes (if bad) | $300-600 | 1 | Safety critical |
| Fluids (top off) | $50-100 | 1 | Engine health |
| Windshield wipers | $30-50 | 2 | Cheap improvement |
| Detailing (wash, interior clean) | $100-150 | 2 | Curb appeal |
| Paint touch-up (if major rust) | $200-400 | 2 | Looks matter |
| AC/Heat repair (if broken) | $200-800 | 2 | Comfort, resale |
| Check engine light (diagnostic) | $150 | 2 | Buyer confidence |

**Repairs to SKIP:**
- ❌ Engine rebuild
- ❌ Transmission work
- ❌ Suspension overhaul
- ❌ Interior restoration
- ❌ Repainting entire vehicle
- ❌ Leather seats/upgrades

**Budget Cap**: $1,500 per vehicle
- If repairs exceed $1,500, likely not worth flipping
- Consider: Parts-out, salvage, or donate

### 3.2 Repair Shop Strategy

**Option A: Use Your Mechanic Team**
- You have mechanics from institutional partnerships
- They understand "flip condition" standard
- Can batch vehicles for efficiency
- Build in 15% margin on parts markup

**Option B: Partner with Local Shop**
- Negotiate bulk/discount rates (10%+ discount)
- Send 2-3 vehicles/week
- Pay at end of week (not per vehicle)
- Establish trust for quick turnarounds

**Option C: Hybrid**
- Your team does simple work (oil, filters, brakes, fluids, detailing)
- Outsource complex work (AC, transmission diagnostics)
- Saves 30-40% on labor costs

### 3.3 Repair Timeline

**Typical vehicle**: 5-10 business days from drop-off to ready-to-sell

```
Day 1: Drop off, initial inspection
Day 1-2: Approve repairs, start work
Day 3-5: Major repairs completed
Day 6-7: Final detailing, quality check
Day 8: Ready for sale
```

## 4. SALES & FLIPPING

### 4.1 Pricing Strategy

**Market Research First**
- Use: Kelley Blue Book, NADA Guides, Edmunds
- Check: Local used car prices (Facebook, Craigslist, CarMax)
- Account for: Mileage, condition, market demand

**Pricing Formula**
```
Base KBB Value (fair condition):     $X
Minus: High mileage discount (-10-15%): -$Y
Minus: Cosmetic issues (-5-10%):     -$Z
Equals: Your asking price:            $PRICE

Example:
2015 Honda Civic KBB fair value:     $10,000
Minus 130K miles (high):             -$1,200
Minus body damage/wear:              -$800
Your asking price:                   $8,000
```

**Profit Calculation**
```
Your selling price:        $8,000
Minus acquisition cost:    -$200
Minus repair costs:        -$1,200
Minus selling costs:       -$400 (fees, docs)
─────────────────────────────────
Net profit:                $6,200
```

### 4.2 Sales Channels

#### **Channel 1: Facebook Marketplace (Primary)**
- ✅ Free listing
- ✅ Reach local buyers (Phoenix metro)
- ✅ Easy communication via Messenger
- ✅ Photos/description clear
- ⚠️ Must respond quickly (competitive market)

**Strategy**:
- List on Friday (weekend buyers)
- Price $300-500 below market (quick sale)
- Highlight: "Recently serviced, runs great, priced to sell"
- Expected time-to-sale: 3-5 days

#### **Channel 2: Craigslist**
- ✅ High local traffic
- ✅ Slightly older buyer demographic
- ✅ Less flaky than FB
- ⚠️ Safety concerns (vet buyers)

**Strategy**:
- List at market price (Craigslist shoppers expect negotiation)
- Price for $200-300 margin on top (they'll negotiate down)
- Require: Pre-approval or proof of funds
- Meet at shop (safe location)

#### **Channel 3: Local Used Car Dealer**
- Offer wholesale: "I'll sell you 2-3 vehicles/month at $500-1,000 under retail"
- Faster sale (no marketing needed)
- Lower profit but guaranteed
- Good for vehicles that aren't selling fast

#### **Channel 4: Carvana/Vroom/CarMax Trade-In**
- Get instant offers online
- Offer to sell your inventory to them
- Lower profit but very fast
- Use as fallback for slow-moving vehicles

### 4.3 Sales Process

**Step 1: List Vehicle**
- Take 10-15 photos (exterior, interior, engine, features)
- Write compelling description (highlight recent service)
- Price competitively ($300-500 under market)
- Post on Facebook + Craigslist simultaneously

**Step 2: Manage Inquiries**
- Respond within 1 hour of inquiry
- Answer basic questions (mileage, condition, price flexibility)
- Schedule test drives (weekends best)
- Vet buyers: "What's the situation? Cash or financing?"

**Step 3: Test Drive**
- Meet at your shop (safe, professional)
- Have buyer bring ID, proof of insurance
- Use: Vehicle history report (show it's clean)
- Answer all questions honestly
- Don't oversell

**Step 4: Close**
- If interested, get deposit ($500-1,000) to hold vehicle
- Require: Proof of funds or financing approval
- Paperwork: Bill of sale, title transfer, odometer statement
- Payment: Cashier's check or bank transfer (not cash)

**Step 5: Handoff**
- Transfer title
- Provide: All service records you have
- Give: 30-day basic warranty (powertrain only)
- Collect positive review (Facebook/Craigslist)

### 4.4 Sales Timeline

```
Friday:    List vehicle (online)
Saturday:  3-5 inquiries expected
Sunday:    Schedule test drives (1-2 scheduled)
Monday:    First test drive
Tuesday:   Buyer decides, puts deposit down
Wednesday: Paperwork completed
Thursday:  Title transferred, payment received
Friday:    Vehicle sold (7 days from listing)
```

## 7. RISK MITIGATION

### 7.1 Common Risks & Solutions

**Risk: Vehicle has hidden damage**
- Solution: Mechanic thoroughly inspects before purchase
- Solution: Get pre-purchase inspection for expensive vehicles
- Solution: Walk away from deals with uncertainty

**Risk: Repairs exceed estimate**
- Solution: Cap at $1,500 budget
- Solution: Cut losses if major issues found
- Solution: Consider salvage/parts-out if needed

**Risk: Vehicle doesn't sell quickly**
- Solution: Price more aggressively (drop $300-500)
- Solution: Offer "as-is" wholesale to dealers
- Solution: Use multiple sales channels simultaneously

**Risk: Buyer disputes or returns**
- Solution: Offer limited 30-day powertrain warranty (not all-inclusive)
- Solution: Clearly disclose all known issues
- Solution: Use bill of sale stating "as-is, no returns"

**Risk: Title/lien issues**
- Solution: Always check title status before purchase
- Solution: Get title in hand before paying
- Solution: Use title company for institutional bulk purchases
