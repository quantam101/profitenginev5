# Institutional Fleet Maintenance Outreach Playbook

## Goal
Establish fleet maintenance retainer partnerships with 5-10 nonprofit + government organizations in Phoenix by Q3 2026, generating $150K-$200K recurring annual revenue.

---

## 1. TARGET PROFILE

### High-Priority Targets (Contact First)
1. **City of Phoenix Public Works** - 500+ vehicle fleet
2. **Phoenix Rescue Mission** - 12-15 vehicles, stable funding
3. **St. Vincent de Paul Phoenix** - 8-12 vehicles, recurring needs
4. **Boys & Girls Clubs Valley** - 8-12 vehicles, 30+ locations
5. **Tempe Union High School** - 25-40 buses + support

### Decision-Maker Profile
- **Title**: Fleet Manager, Operations Director, Executive Director
- **Pain Points**: High maintenance costs, unpredictable budgets, downtime impact
- **Goals**: Cost predictability, vendor reliability, mission alignment
- **Concerns**: Service quality, emergency support, compliance tracking

### Qualify IF:
- ✅ Fleet size: 5-35 vehicles

## 7. OBJECTION HANDLING

### Objection 1: "We use [Vendor] currently"
**Response**:
```
"That makes sense. Most organizations have an existing vendor. The 
question isn't really about switching, it's about whether you're happy 
with what you're paying and the level of service you're getting.

We typically find nonprofits are overpaying by 30-40% because retail 
vendors build in high margins. Would it be worth exploring what you 
could save? There's no risk—if you're happy with your current vendor, 
you don't need to change anything."
```

### Objection 2: "We're locked into a contract"
**Response**:
```
"What's the end date on that contract? Even if you can't switch today, 
let's plan for when it ends. We can walk you through what a transition 
would look like, and you can evaluate us against other vendors when 
you're ready to renew."
```

### Objection 3: "We don't have budget for this"
**Response**:
```
"I hear you. But here's the thing—you're already spending this money on 
maintenance. We're not adding new expense; we're restructuring what you're 
already spending to be more predictable and lower-cost.

For example, if you're spending $18K/year on maintenance right now, you'd 
pay us roughly $12K-$14K instead, depending on your fleet. That's a 
reduction, not an addition to your budget."
```

### Objection 4: "We need to get board/committee approval"
**Response**:
```
"Absolutely, I totally understand the approval process. Here's what I'd 
suggest: let me prepare a brief summary showing the cost savings potential. 
You can present that to the board/committee. If they're interested, we 
can have a follow-up meeting where I answer any technical questions.

When does the board typically meet? Let's work backward from there to 
ensure the timeline works."
```

### Objection 5: "What if you go out of business?"
**Response**:
```
"Great question. A few things:

1) Either of us can terminate with 60 days notice—you're never locked in.

2) All your maintenance records are yours. If we part ways, any successor 
vendor gets full documentation of what's been done.

3) We carry $2M+ in liability insurance and work with multiple 
organizations simultaneously, so we're financially stable.

4) Let's start with a 3-month pilot. See how it goes, and then commit to 
a 12-month contract if you're happy."
```

## 8. NEGOTIATION STRATEGY

### 8.1 Pricing Flexibility
- Base retainer: Non-negotiable (covers our costs)
- Labor rate: Flexible (offer volume discounts for 20+ vehicles)
- Parts markup: Minimal (we make money on volume, not parts)
- Mission donation: Always 5% (competitive advantage)

### 8.2 Contract Terms
- Standard: 12 months auto-renew
- Compromise: 6 months + 6 months (mutual evaluation)
- Pilot: 3 months pilot before full commitment

### 8.3 Concessions (In Order of Preference)
1. **Extended payment terms** (net 45 instead of net 30)
2. **Small discount on retainer** (5-10% volume discount if 20+ vehicles)
3. **Bundled services** (add roadside assistance at no extra cost)
4. **Phased implementation** (add vehicles gradually vs. all at once)
